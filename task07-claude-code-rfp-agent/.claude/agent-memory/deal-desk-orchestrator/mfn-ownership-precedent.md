On the Acme run, Legal's independent read of the MFN warranty (3.4) was
"negotiable, narrow the scope" — a reasonable legal-risk mitigation. Pricing's
playbook has a standing "concessions we will not make" entry for MFN
(hard no / walk-away). When cross-examined, Legal explicitly deferred: MFN is
fundamentally a commercial-term severity call, and Legal's own memory already
noted this was "Pricing's call" before this run even started. Legal
reclassified their own flag from negotiable to blocker and dropped the
narrowed-scope counter from the customer-facing ask (kept in reserve only as
a leadership-authorized last resort).

**Reusable rule:** when a clause sits at the legal/commercial boundary (MFN
pricing warranties are the recurring example; could extend to things like
audit-cost allocation or termination refund mechanics) and Legal and Pricing
produce different severities, default to asking Pricing's playbook to govern
the *lead* position, and let Legal's alternative live as a documented fallback
requiring explicit escalation sign-off rather than presenting two competing
asks to the customer. This isn't "Pricing always wins" — it's "the specialist
whose domain the clause commercially belongs to sets the lead ask; the other
specialist's alternative becomes contingency, not a parallel offer."

made this worth raising in the first place.
