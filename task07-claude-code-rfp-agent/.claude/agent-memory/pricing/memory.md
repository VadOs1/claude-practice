# Pricing Specialist — carryforward memory

## Patterns worth remembering (not deal-specific numbers)

- **RFP-mandated discount floors above our strategic max**: when a customer's
  RFP states a mandatory minimum discount (e.g. "no less than X% off list")
  that exceeds our >$1M strategic band ceiling, don't chase it. Anchor at the
  ceiling, use a closest comparable large deal to show precedent, and be
  explicit that going further requires either (a) real strategic value we
  don't yet have evidence of (reference-ability, target vertical logo,
  genuine multi-year committed volume), or (b) walking away. The Pied Piper
  loss is the cautionary tale: chasing a price-sensitive buyer past our floor
  cost the deal to a competitor who went even lower — we should have walked
  earlier rather than incrementally conceding.

- **Fixed pricing across a committed term + an uncommitted option term**:
  when a customer wants pricing locked for years beyond the committed term
  (e.g. a renewal *option*, not an obligation), don't treat it the same as
  pricing the committed years. The customer holds a one-sided option (walk if
  prices drop, invoke fixed price if prices rise) — quote committed years
  firm, and quote option years as indicative/non-binding or with an
  escalator reserved specifically for the option period, even if the
  customer asked for "no escalators" overall. Flag this explicitly rather
  than silently complying — it's an asymmetric risk, not a rounding issue.

- **Net 90 (or any Net 60+ for non-strong-credit accounts)**: always counter
  with Net 60 as the concession ceiling, framed as a credit-based exception,
  not a norm. Never let Net 90 pass silently.

- **MFN clauses**: always a hard no regardless of deal size or strategic
  logo value — flag as walk-away/escalation item, not a negotiable. Offer a
  narrower substitute (e.g., a renewal-time pricing review) instead of ever
  agreeing to an ongoing "no less favourable than any comparable customer"
  warranty.

- **Termination for convenience with no ETF**: generally acceptable per
  playbook (pro-rated refund only, no penalty), but push notice period up
  (30 days is thin for annual-upfront billing on large accounts) and make
  explicit that "no ETF" does not mean refund of already-delivered service
  period.

- **When escalating discount is tied to term length (playbook's 3-year =
  +5% discount but requires CPI+2% escalator)**: watch for customers who
  want both the multi-year discount AND a no-escalator clause — that
  combination isn't available under the playbook as written; call out the
  conflict explicitly in the recommendation rather than picking one silently.

## Reusable comparables to reach for
- Large industrial/IoT profile similar to Acme -> Initech Sensors (asked
  25%, we held 15%) and Stark Industries (5-year deal, 28%, refused full IP
  assignment but offered licence-back) are the two most useful anchors for
  large industrial accounts pushing on discount + IP/termination terms.
- Pied Piper (lost) is the reusable anchor for "know when to stop conceding
  on price."
