Deal Desk verdict — read before evaluation

Price floor mismatch. The RFP makes a 35% discount off list a mandatory contractual precondition (RFP 3.3, "the successful vendor must offer no less than 35%"). Our proposal opens at 20% and caps at 25% with a reference-agreement concession — 10 points below Acme's stated floor with no path shown to close that gap on price alone. Unless Acme's evaluation genuinely weighs 5-year TCO over headline discount (as the RFP's own 25%/20% commercial/TCO weighting suggests it might), this deal risks losing on price before contractual terms are even discussed.

Blocker exposure is countered, not closed. Of the 8 BLOCKER-severity clauses in the RFP's risk register, 1 (MFN) is flatly rejected and the other 4 (uncapped liability, uncapped regulatory-fine indemnification, IP assignment, immediate-termination-on-SLA-miss) are countered at 50% residual risk, not eliminated. Acme has not yet agreed to any of these counters — each remains a live negotiation item that could reopen at signature and each is exactly the kind of clause procurement-led RFPs are least willing to move on. Net risk lands at 9.0%, squarely in the 5–10% "Escalate to VP" band, not the <5% "Proceed" band.

Deal context — pricing, technical fit & competitive intel

Pricing

Enterprise tier, $720K/yr list. Playbook ceiling for this deal size is 25% (strategic, VP sign-off) against a 15% open / 18–20% target settle band. Acme's mandatory 35% floor sits 10+ points above our ceiling — reject as a contractual precondition, win on 5-year TCO instead. Net 90 countered to Net 60 (Net 90 + annual prepaid + 30-day termination-for-convenience would otherwise open a real cash-risk window). MFN is existential if granted and is rejected outright. 5-year fixed pricing (no escalator) absorbs 100% of infra-cost inflation risk given ~12TB/month growth — priced into the discount band rather than passed back to Acme. Databricks reportedly discounted to 40% on a comparable lost deal (Pied Piper Data) — the competitive field is price-aggressive.

Technical fit — HIGH

Fully met: lakehouse/open formats, batch ETL, Power BI (our strongest, non-negotiable-requirement match), EU residency, multi-region, BI scale, self-service prep, ML foundation. Two live risks: (a) 80K events/sec peak ingest is proven at 250K/sec but only in single-region benchmarks — no validated multi-region ingest test under the EU-residency constraint; mitigated by a Phase 1 joint architecture validation before SLA go-live. (b) 99.99% ask vs. our 99.95% standard tier, compounded by the RFP's zero-cure-period termination remedy — the same clause Legal flags as a BLOCKER.

Competitive landscape

Databricks — primary threat: TCO/compute-cost risk at Acme's scale, no native Power BI. Microsoft Fabric — incumbent threat: Power BI native, Azure/E5 relationship, but only ~18 months in market and real-time ingest isn't a strength. Snowflake — weakest fit: batch-oriented, ML bolted on, typically most expensive at scale. Wedge: TCO predictability + workload integration breadth + platform maturity — avoid competing head-on for "most advanced ML" or "tightest Power BI," where the story is integration, not a single capability.

Blocker concentration

8 of 12 risk-register clauses are BLOCKER or HIGH severity — an unusually dense concentration for one RFP. Only MFN is fully resolved (rejected). The other three BLOCKERs (liability, IP, SLA-termination) and the sole BLOCKER-adjacent indemnification item are all countered, not accepted by Acme yet — each is a live item legal must hold the line on through final contracting, not a closed risk.
